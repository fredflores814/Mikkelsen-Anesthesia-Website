Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about our mobile anesthesia services

Mobile anesthesia brings hospital-level anesthesia care directly to your dental, oral surgery, or medical office. Instead of traveling to a hospital or surgery center, you receive safe, in-office sedation and anesthesia — with the same standards of care.

With mobile anesthesia, you can be treated in the familiar surroundings of your provider's office while still receiving hospital-grade safety standards. The result is more flexible scheduling, less stress for patients, and a smoother, more efficient experience for everyone involved.

I focus exclusively on office-based and mobile anesthesia. That specialization means deeper expertise in outpatient settings, tighter coordination with referring providers, and anesthesia care tailored to each individual patient — safety, efficiency, and personalized attention are my only focus.

Yes — office-based anesthesia is safe when it's delivered by experienced professionals using hospital-grade equipment and monitoring. As a board-certified CRNA trained in outpatient settings, I evaluate each patient carefully beforehand and monitor them continuously before, during, and after the procedure.

I follow nationally recognized standards for office-based anesthesia care, monitoring, and emergency preparedness.

I stay with you for the entire procedure, continuously tracking vital signs like heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen levels, and breathing. Your safety is my sole responsibility from the moment you arrive until you're ready to go home.

I bring hospital-grade anesthesia equipment to every case — advanced monitoring, airway management tools, emergency medications, and oxygen delivery systems — all meeting or exceeding current safety standards.

Your procedure takes place in your provider's office or another designated outpatient setting. I bring all the anesthesia equipment, medications, and monitoring tools directly to that location.

I offer a complete range of anesthesia options, including:

  • Local anesthesia (in coordination with your provider)
  • Monitored Anesthesia Care and IV Sedation
  • Regional Anesthesia
  • General anesthesia

Which option we use depends on your procedure, medical history, comfort level, and the plan we develop together with your provider and anesthesia team.

Depending on your needs, I provide IV sedation, deep sedation, general anesthesia, and regional anesthesia — all appropriate for office-based and outpatient procedures.

It depends on the anesthesia plan we choose together. With IV sedation you'll be relaxed but still responsive; with deep sedation or general anesthesia you'll be fully asleep. I'll review the safest, most appropriate option with you before your procedure.

We base the decision on the procedure itself, your medical history, ASA classification, anxiety level, and recovery considerations. I work closely with your referring provider to recommend the safest and most effective anesthesia plan.

Recovery depends on the type of anesthesia used. Most patients feel groggy or tired at first, with the effects gradually fading over a few hours. The majority go home the same day and are back to normal activities within 24 hours — though you'll need a responsible adult to drive you home and stay with you briefly.

You'll be monitored in the office during recovery until you're stable. Most patients go home the same day, and I'll provide post-anesthesia care instructions to support a safe recovery.
